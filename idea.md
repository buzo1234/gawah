## Back Story

India's new labour codes took effect on 21st November 2025 - exactly one year ago today.

It brought in four new Labour codes covering wages, industrial relations, social security and occupational safety, health and working conditions.

### Did it impact?

It had a mixed impact.
It expanded long term social security and formal protections.
It reduced immediate take-home pay and loosened job security rules.

Lower take-home pay actually means higher savings - more into PF.

Gratuity for fixed-term employees - pro-rata after just 1 year (permanent employees still need 5 years).

Faster exit pay - wages due must be paid by the employer within 2 working days of an employee's resignation, dismissal, or retrenchment.

Paper became mandatory - every employee must get a written appointment letter, and in a wage dispute it is the employer who has to prove the wages were paid.

### Where the codes stop

All of this works for people who have an employer and a payslip.

The codes are built around establishments. A homeowner hiring a painter, or a small contractor hiring three helpers for a week, is mostly outside them. (Our reading - needs a lawyer's check.)

So one year on, the law has told formal India: write it down. The rest of India still makes its deals on a spoken word - and that is where trust breaks.

## Where's the pain today?

Problem is on both sides - workers go unpaid, and people paying them get cheated too.

### Evidence of the Problem

- Most workers are informal: Estimates put 80% to 90% of India's workforce in informal employment. [SBI Research](https://sbi.bank.in/documents/13958/14472/08052026_PLFS_SBI+RESEARCH.pdf/e97cfeb9-95fb-cf06-19f3-029ac971fd5e?t=1778220646800)

- Even salaried jobs are mostly unwritten: About 58% of regular wage and salaried employees have no written job contract. [Labour force survey data](https://dataful.in/datasets/20479/)

## The Idea

A connected system across calling and messaging that sits on the call between a hirer and a worker.

We focus on deals made remotely, over the phone. Deals made in person are out of scope for now - telling apart who is speaking on one phone is its own problem.

How the call starts: one party messages Gawah on WhatsApp with the other person's number. Gawah calls both and connects them. Each person is on their own line, so Gawah always knows who said what.

Two agents work on the call, speaking with one voice:

- Witness - listens to the deal and keeps a record that both sides can rely on.
- Judge - neutral to both sides, and proactive. It knows what a complete contract needs, tracks what has been said so far, and writes the contract.

### On the call

The Judge does not wait to be asked. Before the call ends, it speaks up in its own voice and asks about anything still pending - one question at a time, to the person who has to answer it.

It only asks questions. It never suggests a term or takes a side - that is what keeps it neutral.

What a contract must have:

- The work - what exactly is to be done
- The amount - and what it covers (labour only, or materials too)
- The advance - how much, and when
- The balance - when it is paid, and against what
- The dates - start and finish
- What happens if the work is late or left unfinished

Once nothing is pending, it reads the full terms back to both before they hang up.

### After the call

It calls both the parties independently to explain the terms. Post which - the Judge writes the contract and sends it to both the parties.

### If someone disagrees

- On the call-back, Gawah confirms each term separately (the work, the amount, the advance, the dates). Terms that both sides confirm are locked.
- If someone disputes a term, Gawah plays them the clip from the call where that term was said.
- If they still disagree, Gawah reconnects both parties to settle only that term, then confirms it again with each.
- Nothing is final until both confirm every term. If they can't agree, Gawah tells both that there is no deal on record.

### System of records (core)

The witness stays for the whole job, not just the first call. Otherwise we are solving only half of the story.

Records come in on WhatsApp, because both sides already use it - no new app, less friction.

- Either party sends a record on WhatsApp: an advance, a payment, a day of work, a finished milestone.
- Backed records - the proof stands on its own (eg: a UPI payment screenshot with a transaction ID). Logged directly, no confirmation needed.
- Unbacked records - one person's word (eg: "paid ₹2,000 in cash" or "work is finished"). The other party has to confirm.
- For unbacked records, the WhatsApp bot asks the other party first. Gawah calls them only if they don't reply.
- For the demo, we fix upfront which record types count as backed and which don't.

## Extensions
