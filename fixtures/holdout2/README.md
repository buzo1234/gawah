# Held-out calls, set 2

Six calls written without looking at the app, its tests, its saved answers, the practice calls or the first held-out set (only the key layout of one practice file was read, to copy the format). The trades, people, number forms and endings differ from everything Gawah was built on. They use the practice-call format, so `fakes/replayer` plays them as they are, and they carry the same extra fields as the first held-out set: `noise` (`clean` or `stt`), `wrap_up_starts_line`, `pending_at_end`, and `must_stay_silent` (lines after which the Judge must not speak although they sound final or look like a problem). Each `expected` entry has `after_line`, `by_line`, `item` (`null` for a plain read-back), `reason`, `to`, `modes`, `required` (false means a reasonable listener could go either way) and `note`, and sometimes `reason_also_ok` or `also_pending`.

New in this set:

- `call_no`, `deal` and `call_date` on every file. `call_date` anchors words like "kal" and "Monday".
- Two `reason` values beyond `term_unclear`, `conflicting_figures`, `contradicts_record` and `wrapping_up`: `two_meanings` (an agreement a careful listener could hear two ways; `term_unclear` is also accepted) and `question_to_gawah` (someone asks Gawah by name; the entry has an `answer` field with the correct reply).
- `noise_notes` on the speech-to-text calls, listing each mis-hearing and split line.
- `previous_call` and `record_before_call` on the second call of the pair: what both agreed on call 1, which the contradiction is measured against. Replay `mason_wall_call1.json` first, then `mason_wall_call2.json` into the same deal (`--deal-id`).

The brain and the Scribe have no saved answers for these calls, so a replay gets no Judge output unless it runs live (`BRAIN_LIVE_REPLAYS=1` and `MEMORY_LIVE_REPLAYS=1`: one billed Sage2 update and one small-model call per line). The six calls are 198 lines in all.

**tailor_lehenga.json**: an anxious mother of the bride and a calm old tailor settle a wedding order in romanised Hinglish: lehenga blouse and fitting plus four suits, quoted as "saade chaar hazaar" and "baarah sau ek, chaar ke hue adtaalis sau" in one breath, summed by her as "nau hazaar teen sau" and bargained to nine thousand. It tests a price "with material" that can be heard two ways: he says at line 6 that she brings the lining, then answers "haan ji, saaman milake" to "saara saaman milake na? Latkan, dori, sab?" while talking about latkan. Asking at once is optional; she raises the lining again in the goodbye ("ya aap le aaoge?"), so the wrap-up must ask it. Her direct question about what happens if he misses the 5th is dodged with a story and "tension mat lo", which is a required question. She asks "Gawah ji ... advance kitna bola, do hazaar hi na?" (answer: 2,000; "aadha" was only his first ask). It ends in a rambling goodbye with a false ending at line 31 and a reopened item at 33.

**driver_monthly.json**: speech-to-text in Devanagari with English in Latin script, no punctuation, "साढ़े आठ" heard as "साड़ी आठ", one sentence split over lines 15 and 16, and a last line cut off mid-word. A brisk young professional and a polite driver settle salary, hours, overtime and start date, with acceptance buried mid-line ("अठारह ok चलो ठीक है तो कल से आ जाओ"). "No advance" is a settled answer, so asking about the advance is wrong. Sunday pay gets a filler "ठीक है ठीक है वो देखते हैं" that agrees to nothing, which is a required question. Leaving mid-way is raised and answered with a reference, not a term (optional live, required at wrap-up). It ends abruptly: "मेरा दूसरा call आ रहा है", "bye", and the call is gone. The Judge has only the pause after line 30 or 31.

**mason_wall_call1.json**: first call of the pair. A curt retired soldier and a talkative mason settle a 180-foot boundary wall: "do lakh das hazaar" against "dedh lakh", settled at "paune do lakh, material samet" with the materials listed, so "with material" is not vague here. Then "pachaas, pachhattar, pachaas" instalments that add up to the total, 10 to 31 December, 1,000 a day after the 31st not counting rain days, and pay for work done if he leaves. All six items are settled by line 25, followed by chat about their sons and a clean goodbye. It tests that the Judge stays silent through bargaining, instalment figures and a "January" that is not a date term, then gives one final read-back. A read-back after line 25 is optional.

**mason_wall_call2.json**: second call, two days into the work. Inside a reassuring line ("main kuch extra nahi maang raha ... tab sava lakh ka intezaam rakhna") the mason puts 1,25,000 due at shoulder height, against the 75,000 both agreed on call 1. The hirer says "haan haan, intezaam ho jayega" without noticing, so the Judge must raise it from the record. Then a change both agree to: the 60-foot road-side stretch ("saath foot lambi") goes up to 7 feet ("saat foot"), bargained 20,000, 12,000, 15,000, added to the last instalment ("pachaas plus pandrah, painsath"). That must be recorded, not flagged. The hirer asks "Gawah ... khatam kab tak, aur uske baad kitna roz?" (answer: 31 December, 1,000 a day excluding rain). The mason repeats "agle hafte sava lakh" at the close and hangs up when a truck arrives. The wrap-up must name 1,25,000 against 75,000 and use the changed terms (total 1,90,000, last instalment 65,000).

**ac_repair.json**: speech-to-text in Devanagari, with "ढाई हज़ार" heard as "दाई हज़ार", the brand Voltas as "वॉल्टर्स", and one sentence split over lines 22 and 23. A polite retired teacher and a fast-talking young technician agree a service of two ACs for a thousand (asked twelve hundred), gas at 2,500 if needed, tomorrow by 10:30, no advance, cash after, and a gas warranty. It tests a scope that can be heard two ways: "bedroom वाला भी देख लेना साथ में", "हां हां वो भी देख लूंगा ... उसी में" can mean a free look or a free service of the third AC, and line 13 makes a service free only if gas is filled. The Judge should ask (required). A compressor replacement is priced (8,000) and deferred on purpose by both; noting it is optional and treating it as a conflict is wrong. Fillers ("हां हां ठीक है") and the corrected gas price must not draw a question. It ends with "बाकी बाद में बात करते हैं" and talk about the address and stairs.

**car_mechanic.json**: romanised Hinglish with digits, English number words ("twelve hundred", "eighty seven hundred") and Hindi ones in the same exchange. An impatient sales manager and an unhurried garage owner agree a clutch, brake and service job: 8,700, then 9,300 with extras, then "9,000 flat", accepted by moving on. It tests "Monday tak de dunga" said to a man who must leave for Kanpur on Monday: before he leaves, or some time on Monday? The cab-fare penalty, said laughing and then confirmed as "pakka", depends on which. The Judge should ask (required, and again at wrap-up if not settled). "No advance" is settled. A filler "haan theek hai, theek hai" on new tyres agrees to nothing, so the tyres must not appear in a read-back. He asks "Gawah, total kitna fix hua tha, 9,000 ya 9,300?" (answer: 9,000 flat). Cricket chat follows once everything is settled, then a short goodbye.
